# Evidence Trail - Condensed

**Source:** Alien Intelligence MCP connector interacting with the OpenAIRE Graph, August 2026.  
**Important:** counts below are approximate pagination totals returned by the MCP workflow. They are query- and time-dependent and are not presented as systematic-review counts.

## Investigation 1 - Field map
- Broad AI-literacy mapping identified foundational frameworks and nine broad strands.
- The output treated Long & Magerko (2020) and Ng et al. (2021) as major anchors.
- Key methodological lesson: the OpenAIRE Graph was used for bibliometric classes, research relationships and author/citation structure, not only keyword retrieval.
- A broad publication-growth estimate was returned, but the project did **not** treat this raw trend as a core finding without qualification because match logic can be expansive.

## Investigation 2 - Epistemic competencies
Approximate returned counts included:
- AI literacy + critical thinking: ~2,223
- AI literacy + reasoning: ~806
- AI literacy + interpretation + explainability: ~25
- AI literacy + judgment + evaluation: ~134
- AI literacy + uncertainty + probabilistic: ~18

Interpretive pattern:
- Critical evaluation is structurally present in frameworks.
- Human reasoning, interpretation and judgment are less consistently operationalised as distinct AI-literacy research objects.
- Uncertainty/probabilistic reasoning was the sparsest intersection.

## Investigation 3 - Mathematical/statistical/probabilistic reasoning
- Mathematical reasoning links were largely inferred or population-based rather than defined as AI-literacy competencies.
- Mathematical literacy showed little direct connection to the mainstream AI-literacy framework tradition.
- Model-based reasoning appeared nascent but more structurally compatible with AI-literacy frameworks.
- Probabilistic reasoning had the clearest theoretical rationale but very thin follow-through; one query returned 6 records at the tight AI-literacy/probabilistic/uncertainty intersection.
- The analysis did not identify a stable shared author/citation/venue cluster across these concepts.

## Investigation 4 - Judgment-oriented adjacent traditions
Approximate returned counts included:
- automation bias: ~987 records in the tested formulation
- trust calibration: ~92
- appropriate reliance: ~161

Interpretive pattern:
- “Algorithmic judgment” and “epistemic judgment” were not established AI-literacy constructs.
- Trust calibration, appropriate reliance, automation bias and algorithm aversion are established in HCI/psychology/clinical/public-administration literatures.
- These traditions operationalise the behavioural decision to accept, reject, adhere to or override AI advice.
- A direct test combining trust calibration with statistical/probabilistic reasoning returned only noise, suggesting a logically plausible but empirically unexamined bridge.

## Investigation 5 - Falsification test
Selected bridge queries:
- AI literacy + appropriate reliance + trust: 21 returned records, but top results did not constitute the HCI appropriate-reliance tradition.
- AI literacy + automation bias + algorithm aversion: 0 in a strict four-term query; shorter reformulations found a few partial bridge papers.
- probabilistic reasoning + trust calibration + AI uncertainty: 9 records, judged noise.
- mathematical literacy + uncertainty + AI judgment: 0.
- data literacy + AI decision + accept/reject/override: 1 noise record.

Citation/overlap tests:
- Sampled citing networks of AI-literacy and XAI/deployment anchor papers showed no mature cross-community citation bridge in the examined nodes.
- No author was found straddling all three traditions in the investigated outputs.
- No paper was identified that integrated all three: AI-literacy competencies + human-AI reliance/judgment + mathematical/statistical reasoning.

## Strongest partial bridges
The investigation retained partial counter-evidence rather than claiming absolute absence. Examples reported by the workflow included recent work combining AI literacy with algorithm aversion/dependence, but these papers did not yet constitute a mature cross-disciplinary cluster and did not integrate the quantitative reasoning tradition.

## Final bounded claim
**The fragmentation hypothesis was supported within the OpenAIRE-indexed evidence examined.** Isolated bridges exist, but the evidence did not reveal a mature research programme integrating AI-literacy frameworks, human-AI judgment/reliance, and mathematical/statistical reasoning under uncertainty.

## Known limitations
- Partial ScholeXplorer/citation coverage.
- Underindexing of some behavioral-science literature.
- Citation lag for 2025-2026 papers.
- AND-logic query collapse and false abundance.
- Metadata inconsistencies may occur in AI-mediated synthesis; bibliographic details should be verified before formal scholarly citation.
