# Reusable Bridge-Tracing Protocol

## Purpose

A non-code method for using a scholarly knowledge graph to test whether an apparent research gap is a true absence, a vocabulary mismatch, or a fragmented cross-disciplinary landscape.

## Step 1 - Map the home field
Start with a neutral broad query. Identify anchor papers, recurring authors, major constructs, venues and growth patterns. Do not insert the hypothesised gap into the first query.

## Step 2 - Test implicit competencies separately
Search each candidate competence individually (e.g., reasoning, interpretation, uncertainty, judgment). Distinguish explicit occurrence in title/abstract/keywords from inferred conceptual relevance.

## Step 3 - Trace adjacent traditions
If the home field names a competence but does not operationalise it, search the discipline where the behaviour is measured or theorised. For this case, “critical evaluation” led to HCI/psychology terms such as appropriate reliance and trust calibration.

## Step 4 - Inspect relationship evidence
Use multiple relationship types where possible: publication details, citation networks, author profiles, research relationships, trend analysis, project searches and venue overlap. A keyword co-occurrence alone is not treated as integration.

## Step 5 - Classify candidate bridges
A candidate bridge is stronger when it shows:
1. **Theoretical engagement** with constructs from both traditions.
2. **Methodological inheritance** from both traditions.
3. **Citation mass / influence** beyond an isolated new paper.
4. **Community formation** - multiple bridge papers citing one another.

## Step 6 - Search for counter-evidence
For every emerging claim, ask the system to find evidence that would make the claim false or substantially weaker. Record genuine counter-evidence rather than explaining it away.

## Step 7 - Run a final falsification test
Search all plausible bridge vocabularies and relationship types. Compare genuine integration with incidental term overlap. State the strongest partial bridges even if the main interpretation survives.

## Step 8 - Report with bounded claims
Separate:
- what is established,
- what is only inferred,
- what partial bridges exist,
- what was not found,
- and what the retrieval/indexing limitations mean for confidence.

## Stopping rule
Stop when additional searches no longer change the structural interpretation and the main claim has survived targeted counter-searches.

## Failure modes to document
- AND-logic query collapse on long queries.
- False abundance when terms co-occur incidentally.
- Ambiguity between human reasoning and AI-system reasoning.
- Incomplete citation-network/indexing coverage.
- Citation lag for recent papers.
- Metadata inconsistencies in AI-mediated synthesis.

## Reuse
Replace the three traditions in this case with the “home field,” “adjacent operational tradition,” and “candidate foundational reasoning tradition” for another interdisciplinary question. Keep the falsification and bounded-claim steps unchanged.
